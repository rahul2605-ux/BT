\section{Methodology} \label{sec:method}

The study compares a learned jammer with parametric jammers at matched damage, against each detector, in each environment.
This section describes the learned jammer, how it is trained, and how every jammer is scored.

\subsection{Learned Jammer} \label{sec:method:generator}
The learned jammer is a generator network $G$ that maps a latent vector $\mathbf{u}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$ to a segment $G(\mathbf{u})\in\mathbb{C}^{M}$ of complex baseband samples, with the layout of Zhou et al.~\cite{11184988}.
Its transmission is a stream of independent segments, delayed and rotated asynchronously and scaled to the budget of~\eqref{eq:power},
\begin{equation*}
c[n]=a\,e^{\mathrm{j}\phi}\,v[n-\tau],\qquad v=\bigl[G(\mathbf{u}_1),G(\mathbf{u}_2),\ldots\bigr],
\end{equation*}
with $(\tau,\phi)$ the per-frame offset and phase of Section~\ref{sec:sysmodel:jammer} and $a>0$ the amplitude that meets the budget.
Each segment is one frame long, $M=NL$, and keeps a fixed position relative to the victim's frames before the offset $\tau$, so the generator can place its power on the same symbol of every frame.
The generator therefore uses the frame timing, which places it in tier T2, and never observes the victim's symbols.

\subsection{Training} \label{sec:method:training}
The generator starts from random weights and learns only from a differentiable form of Problem~\ref{opt_problem:jammer}, back-propagated through the link and the frozen detector; no discriminator is trained.
Two quantities are replaced by surrogates.
The expected \ac{ber} is numerically zero wherever the jammer is weak, so the objective uses $\log\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{BER}]$, computed exactly over the receiver noise.
A bit whose noiseless matched-filter sample lies at signed distance $m_b$ from its decision boundary is in error with probability $Q(m_b/\sigma)$, with $\sigma^2=\nu N_0/2$, and a log-sum-exp evaluates the logarithm of the mean without underflow.
The detection probability is replaced by
\begin{equation}
\tilde P_\text{det}=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(1+e^{-(T(r)-\tau_\alpha)/s_T}\bigr)^{-1}\Bigr],
\label{eq:softpdet}
\end{equation}
with $s_T$ the standard deviation of $T$ on clean frames, which tends to $P_\text{det}$ as $s_T\to0$.
The classifier maps its spectrogram onto a colour map with a finite number of levels, whose gradient is zero; a straight-through estimator~\cite{bengio2013estimating} keeps the exact image in the forward pass and uses the colour map's slope in the backward pass, so the gradient reaches the generator through the classifier's own weights.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \DontPrintSemicolon
    \KwInput{detector statistic $T$, threshold $\tau_\alpha$, clean spread $s_T$, weight $\beta$, \ac{jsr} band $[J_\text{lo},J_\text{hi}]$, steps $S$}
    \KwOutput{generator $G$}
    Initialise $G$ with random weights\;
    \For{$i\in\{1,\ldots,S\}$}{
        Draw $J$ uniformly in decibels from $[J_\text{lo},J_\text{hi}]$\; \label{alg:train:jsr}
        Draw a batch of frames: bits, $\rho$, $\nu$, noise, latents $\mathbf{u}$, offsets $(\tau,\phi)$\;
        $c\gets$ the stream of $G(\mathbf{u})$, delayed, rotated and scaled to $J$ by~\eqref{eq:power}\; \label{alg:train:power}
        $r\gets\rho\,x+c+w$\;
        $\mathcal{L}\gets-\log\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{BER}]+\beta\,\tilde P_\text{det}$\; \label{alg:train:loss}
        Update $G$ by one Adam step on $\nabla\mathcal{L}$\; \label{alg:train:step}
    }
    \caption{Training of a generator against one detector\label{alg:train}}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:train} lists the training loop.
Each step draws the received \ac{jsr} from a band (Algorithm~\ref{alg:train}, Line~\ref{alg:train:jsr}) that starts below the \ac{jsr} at which the target detector begins to flag white noise in that environment, because where the detector flags every frame the logistic term of~\eqref{eq:softpdet} is flat and carries no gradient.
The power constraint acts inside the forward pass (Line~\ref{alg:train:power}): it removes the generator's overall gain, so the gradient changes only the shape of the waveform, never its power.
The loss (Line~\ref{alg:train:loss}) takes the logarithm of the damage, so $\beta$ has a different numerical range than in Problem~\ref{opt_problem:jammer}, and $\beta=0$ gives a damage-only control that shares everything with the detector-aware generators except the detection term.
The difference between the two is what the detection term buys.
Training draws the environment's own fading and noise-level factors, so each generator is trained for the environment it is scored in.

\subsection{Scoring} \label{sec:method:scoring}
Every jammer is swept over the \ac{jsr}, and at each point the same frames give its damage and each detector's $P_\text{det}$.
The matched-damage detection probability $P^{\star}_\text{det}(d)$ of Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation} is read at the first \ac{jsr} where the damage reaches $d$, interpolated in the logarithm of the damage.
The \textit{parametric envelope} is the lowest $P^{\star}_\text{det}$ that any parametric jammer of a family reaches at that damage, each with its best parameter.
The classifier enters the comparison grey-box: one classifier, the surrogate, sets the generators' training band and trains them, and their detection is scored on independently trained classifiers of the same recipe, the deployed ones.
A white-box score on the surrogate serves as a reference.
Every threshold is set on clean frames of the environment being scored, so all detectors operate at the same \ac{far}.
