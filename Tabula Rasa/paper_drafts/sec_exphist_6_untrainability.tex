\subsection{Untrainability of Policy-Gradient RL over Raw IQ}
\label{subsec:exphist:untrainable}
Direct gradient descent had beaten the hand-designed statistic, but on two assumptions that a learned
defender removes: a differentiable model of the detection statistic, and observation of the symbol
under attack.
A frozen classifier accessed through its scalar output supplies neither, so the attacker has to be
trained by policy gradient, and the question this step asked was whether it can be.

We kept the OFDM link and the frozen detector and replaced the training rule.
Two cooperative flow-based jammers were trained with MAPPO -- a centralised critic over both agents'
observations and actions, actors on local observations only -- each synthesising a complex
per-subcarrier vector injected in the frequency domain, under the reward of \eqref{eq:reward} with the
detection term $-\log(1-\hat{p}+\epsilon)$.

All three runs failed in the same way, with the detector's output pinned at $\hat{p} \approx 0.999$
from initialisation to termination.
With a linearly warmed penalty the power diverged from 4 to 33 while the policy entropy saturated at
its ceiling; reshaping the penalty amplified its magnitude but not its variation; removing the entropy
bonus stopped the power growth and froze the policy outright, with bit rate, entropy and power
unchanged over 290 iterations.
A static probe of the detector explains the pinning: single-subcarrier interference goes unflagged at
power 16 ($\hat{p} \approx 0.007$), while interference spread over every subcarrier is flagged at
power $0.01$ per subcarrier ($\hat{p} = 1.000$).
A normalising flow initialised near $\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})$ over 128 dimensions is broadband noise
by construction, so every action the agent sampled was flagged, and the reward carried no information
about which action was better.

The reward shape makes this worse than a cold start.
A log barrier at $\hat{p} \approx 0.999$ is large, but it is large by almost exactly the same amount
for every element of the batch, and policy gradient normalises advantages within the batch: a large
constant penalty and no penalty at all are the same gradient.
That is a property of the objective rather than a failure of training, and no schedule on $\beta$
changes it.

Reducing the action space to two real dimensions -- one target subcarrier -- separated the two
failures.
Stealth was then solved throughout ($\hat{p} \approx 0.003$, a genuinely varying signal), and waveform
learning still failed: the per-subcarrier bit error rate plateaued at $0.35$ against an attainable
$1.0$, with the sampled distribution an isotropic blob and no correlation between the observed symbol
and the emitted one.
A scalar frame-level reward cannot teach an input--output correlation, because the agent would first
have to sample the correct input-dependent direction by chance and only then reinforce it, and
isotropic exploration in a space of that size does not produce that sample.

The last variant removed the remaining genie assumption, giving the jammer the previous transmitted
symbol in place of the current one.
For i.i.d.\ QPSK that observation carries no information about the symbol under attack, so the
cancellation strategy is unreachable rather than merely hard, and the agent must learn a blind
waveform distribution.
Five runs each repaired the previous run's mechanical failure and each failed identically
(Table~\ref{tab:blackbox}); the policy entropy across all five is flat at $178.4$ to within
$0.05\,\%$.

Two of those rows are worth reading closely.
The mask is a structural prior and not a neutral change: it presupposes that the evasive strategy is
sparse instead of letting the agent discover that, and it is justified only as a feasibility check,
since a flow initialised over 128 dimensions has no pathway to sparse samples and the gradient is flat
before the agent ever reaches one.
The drop from $K=1$ to $\hat{p} \approx 0.4$, rather than the $\approx 0.0002$ the static probe
predicted, is not learning either: the flow resamples at each of the 14 OFDM symbols and may select a
different subcarrier each time, while the detector sees one spectrogram per frame, so a temporally
hopping single tone presents as multi-tone.
A controlled probe reproduces the discrepancy exactly, and holding the action over the frame removes
it.
The apparent threshold at four simultaneously active subcarriers, which we had read as a property of
the detector, is characterised in Section~\ref{subsec:exphist:characterisation} and turns out not to
be one.

What this establishes is a constraint on the attacker's parameterisation rather than on its objective.
Raw in-phase and quadrature samples give a search space in which the reward is constant over the
region the policy can reach, and reward engineering cannot repair that, because the quantity that is
missing is per-dimension credit and not penalty magnitude.
The design that follows from it is a small set of perturbation parameters as the action, and a
gradient wherever one is available in place of a scalar return.
Stated as a result rather than as a failure: unconstrained continuous policy-gradient learning does
not discover sparse evasive strategies from a broadband initialisation without a structural prior, and
an attacker restricted to that route pays a prohibitive adaptation cost.
