Six-Environment Jamming Run

Every attacker against every detector, retrained at Es/N0 24 dB, in six environments: the ideal link, two levels of noise-level uncertainty, two levels of Rician fading, and both together. This page explains what was built and checked, then shows each environment's figures and tables and the comparison across environments.

What was built, and why

The paper's results were spread over a dozen separate probes (E2, S1, S4, S5, D6), each with its own attackers, detectors and operating point. The final experiment puts them into one pipeline: every attacker against every detector, with the detectors and the learned jammers retrained at one realistic operating point, and the same comparison repeated under noise-level uncertainty, under fading, and under both. It answers the supervisor's points of 29.9: Pdet is now defined, outperformance is quantified against literature baselines, his constant-vector attack is in the baseline set, channel uncertainty and time variation are the realism axes, and the modulation is fixed (QPSK).

The code lives in final/. It is a pruned copy of the cgan/ code, never an import (both folders have a link.py). Each copied file names its source and commit on its first line. Per environment, one SLURM chain runs four steps:

  1. train_cnn: retrain Li et al.'s spectrogram CNN on that environment's frames, then calibrate every detector's threshold on 20 000 clean frames of the same environment.
  2. bands: choose the JSR range each learned jammer is trained over, from where white noise starts to be detected in that environment.
  3. train_gan: train eight generators white-box against that environment's detectors.
  4. evaluate: sweep every attacker from −50 to +15 dB JSR (1 dB steps, 512 frames per point) and record damage and every detector's Pdet.

Two things are new relative to cgan/. The energy detector is measured after the matched filter (mean received symbol energy) instead of over the full simulated band, and the victim's link can carry per-frame Rician fading instead of S1's slow log-normal shadowing. Everything else (link, channel, jammers, CNN, training loss) is the copied code, and the regression gate below shows it reproduces S4.

The link and the operating point

One transmitter sends QPSK to one receiver: 128 symbols per frame, root-raised-cosine pulses (roll-off 0.35), 8 samples per symbol, a matched filter and a sign decision per axis. The receiver knows its own carrier phase, so there is no equaliser. One jammer adds its waveform with a random clock offset and carrier phase per frame. Its received power is set exactly to the JSR on every frame (a hard constraint, never a loss term).

SNR 15 dB in the code is Es/N0 24 dB in the paper. The code's SNR is power per sample over the 8× oversampled band, so Es/N0 = SNR + 10 log108 = SNR + 9.03 dB. The literature case is a fixed-QPSK UAS control link operating with about 10 dB of margin (Kakar's C2 link closes at ≈ 14 dB at the edge of its range). All JSR values are received jammer power over received signal power, per sample, over the symbol window.

Pdet and the CFAR rule

Pdet(JSR) = Pr[T(r) > τα | jammer present at that received JSR], with τα the (1 − α) quantile of T over 20 000 clean frames, α = 0.05 for every detector. Estimated on 512 frames per JSR point, per frame (not "ever caught"). Both probabilities run over everything random: bits, noise, the jammer's input, delay and phase, and the channel draws.

CFAR rule. The clean frames that set τα come from the same environment the detector is evaluated in, including everything the defender cannot know (the noise level, the fading). The false-alarm rate therefore stays α and the threshold rises instead. That loss of sensitivity is the realistic cost of not knowing.

Damage is excess BER, SER and PER (frame error rate) over the same environment's clean frames, on the same frames as Pdet. Pdet at matched damage: find the JSR where an attacker first reaches the damage level (interpolated in log damage), and read its Pdet there. The two levels are excess PER 0.1 and excess BER 3·10⁻⁴.

The six environments

All six sit at our SNR 15 dB (Es/N0 24 dB). They differ in what the defender does not know about its own clean signal. Noise-level uncertainty (σN) scales each frame's noise variance by a log-normal factor with that standard deviation in dB, mean 1, which the receiver does not know (S5's model). Fading multiplies each frame of the victim's own signal by a Rician amplitude with unit mean power; the receiver knows its phase, so clean BER stays ≈ 0. Per-frame Pdet and damage depend only on the per-frame distribution of these draws, so drawing them independently per frame changes no reported number (real frames are correlated over milliseconds; a frame is 128 µs at 1 MBd).

The last two columns are measured in this run: how far above the clean mean the energy detector's CFAR threshold had to move (the price of not knowing), and the retrained CNN's validation accuracy and AUC on Li et al.'s own frame classes. For reference, S4/D6's 15 dB CNN (r0) had 0.932 / 0.978.

Training recipe and JSR bands

Recipe (run003's, as in S4): Zhou's generator architecture from random weights, 4000 Adam steps (lr 2·10⁻⁴), 128 frames per step (32 for the CNN target, which keeps a full EfficientNet graph per frame). Each step draws one JSR uniformly from the target's band, simulates frames of that environment (noise factor and fading included), and minimises

loss = −( log E[BER] − β · soft Pdet )

log E[BER] is exact over the noise (each bit's error probability at the noiseless matched-filter sample, with each frame's own noise variance). soft Pdet = mean sigmoid((T − τα)/σclean) at the environment's CFAR threshold. Power never enters the loss: every frame is scaled to exactly its JSR. The generators' seeds follow cgan/'s task ids, so seed 0 of each task is the seed run003 and S4 used.

Why bands matter. If every sampled JSR lies above the target detector's transition, every frame is detected with certainty, the sigmoid is flat, and the detector term gives no gradient whatever β is (trap 1 of §3.1). The band must straddle the transition. The training logs confirm it did: every targeted generator's soft Pdet moves between ≈ 0.13 and ≈ 0.99 across its band.

The rule (S4's, made automatic in bands.py): start from the 30 dB bands (CNN (−48, −16), energy and kurtosis (−32, 0)), and shift each by how far that detector's white-noise transition (Pdet = 0.5) moved between the deployed 30 dB defender and this environment's. Cap the analytic targets' upper edge at 0 dB. The vanilla cGAN (damage only) keeps (−32, 0). At 30 dB the reference transitions are: energy −16.9 dB, full-band power −27.2 dB, kurtosis −13.6 dB, CNN −29.9 dB.

Deviation from the spec. §3.4 assumed energy and full-band power transition within ≈ 1 dB at 30 dB, "so power's reference serves for energy". That holds for an in-band jammer (measured: 1.4 dB apart for matched QPSK), but not for white noise: the matched filter passes only a tenth of a white jammer's frame energy while the clean spread is the same, so energy's white-noise transition sits 10 dB above power's (measured 10.3 dB). Using power's −27 dB reference for energy would have shifted the energy band about 10 dB too high, above the transition. The shift is therefore measured on energy's own statistic at both ends. The result at base, (−25.1, 0), matches S4's power band (−24, 0), which is what the rule is for.

The DC-share diagnostic

Caveats, deviations and failed jobs

Deviations from the spec

  • Energy's 30 dB band reference. The spec said energy and full-band power transition within ≈ 1 dB at 30 dB, so power's reference could serve for energy. That is true for an in-band jammer (1.4 dB apart, measured) and false for white noise, the probe the band rule uses (10.3 dB apart, measured; the matched filter passes a tenth of a white jammer's frame energy). The shift is therefore measured on energy's own statistic at both ends. The result at base matches S4's band. verify tests the physically correct predictions in place of the two README ones, and the "1.35/8 noise share" is corrected to ≈ 1/10 the same way.
  • A simulated on/off exists in attacks.py for verify only. The reported on/off is analytic, as specified. Its silent frames are charged the measured clean FAR of the environment, not exactly α.
  • Extra coverage in the gate: S4's seven classical attacks were compared as well as its four generators.
  • Figures (redrawn 30.9 on request): each detector column draws six attackers, with Amuru as its single best p at excess PER 0.1; all seven p, the envelope and every other attacker are in the tables. Damage and Pdet share one JSR axis instead of two figures. The DC-share figure is a dot plot, since the value does not depend on JSR.
  • The random push replaces the genie push in the figures. A push should not assume the attacker knows the symbols. The genie push stays in the tables.

Caveats

  • The band rule can starve a generator of low-power training. Where the target detector only reacts at high JSR, the band follows it up. The energy-conditioned cGAN under fading trained on (−8, 0) dB and never learned to damage at −20 dB, so it is loud and flagged 1.00 by the CNN. In both, two of three CNN-conditioned seeds (band (−35.8, −3.8)) ended with a damage onset of −11 / −12 dB instead of −21. These are training outcomes, not crashes. Nothing was rerun: that would need a recipe change the spec does not allow. The fair reading of both uses seed r1 (0.054, level with the control).
  • The random push needs synchronisation. It knows the victim's symbol timing and carrier phase, which no asynchronous attacker in the set has. It is a reference between the genie and the real attackers, not a practical jammer. Its per-frame power is capped, so it attacks whole symbols: nothing below −20.2 dB, and PER 0.55–0.72 at −20 dB. Its matched-damage Pdet is therefore read at that first point and overstates what it would need at PER 0.1. Diluting it over frames the way on/off dilutes matched QPSK (our construction, not computed in the pipeline) would bring its energy Pdet at PER 0.1 at base to ≈ 0.10.
  • Amuru's best p sits at the edge of the family. Against the CNN and kurtosis it is 0.01 or 0.02 in every environment. p below 0.01 was not evaluated and might do better still.
  • One CNN per environment, seed 11, no retrain replicate (as §3.3o).
  • One seed for the energy- and kurtosis-conditioned cGANs.
  • energy_csi is handed the true gain. A real receiver estimates it, so it lies between the two energy detectors. No estimator was built.
  • Per-frame independent draws of fading and noise level. This changes no per-frame number, only multi-frame reasoning.
  • Matched damage by interpolation on a 1 dB grid, 512 frames per point: Pdet std ≈ 0.01 near 0.05 and ≈ 0.02 near 0.5 for a single seed.
  • At BER 3·10⁻⁴ the on/off jammer ends the comparison with the classical envelope. It needs one jammed frame in ≈ 1500, so its Pdet is the FAR. Against Amuru's best p alone, which the figures use, that column has room again.
  • Every threshold, CNN and evaluation ran on TITAN RTX. CUDA draws differ between GPU models, so a rerun on another card is statistically, not bitwise, equivalent.
