\section{Problem Formulation} \label{sec:problem_formulation}

Let us define the \textit{jammer law} $\pi$ as the distribution of the jamming waveform $c$ given the information of its tier, and the \textit{damage} $D_\pi(J)$ as the excess \ac{fer} it causes at \ac{jsr} $J$ over clean frames of the same environment.
The jammer solves

\begin{subequations}
\refstepcounter{problem} \label{opt_problem:jammer}
\begingroup
\renewcommand{\theequation}{\theproblem}
\renewcommand{\theparentequation}{\theproblem}
\renewcommand{\theequation}{\theparentequation\alph{equation}}
    \begin{align}
        \underset{\pi}{\text{maximize}} \quad
        & \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{BER}] - \beta\,P_\text{det} \label{pb:objective} \\
        \text{subject to} \quad
        & \frac{1}{NL}\sum_{n}|c[n]|^2=J\,P_\text{rx} \quad \text{for every frame,} \label{cn:power}
    \end{align}
\endgroup
\end{subequations}

\noindent where $\beta\ge0$ weighs detection against damage.
Constraint~\ref{cn:power} (\textit{Power}) is the budget of~\eqref{eq:power}, so power never enters the objective.
Each $\beta$ reaches only the upper concave envelope of the achievable damage--detection pairs, so Problem~\ref{opt_problem:jammer} describes how a jammer is trained, not how jammers are compared.
% README 2.8: reward = BER - beta * detections, power a hard constraint.

Jammers choose their power, so a comparison at equal \ac{jsr} or equal configuration favours whichever jammer happens to be more efficient at that setting.
Let us define the \textit{matched-damage detection probability} of a jammer at damage level $d$ as
\begin{equation*}
P^{\star}_\text{det}(d)=P_\text{det}\bigl(J^{\star}(d)\bigr),\qquad J^{\star}(d)=\min\{J : D_\pi(J)\ge d\},
\end{equation*}
the detection probability at the lowest \ac{jsr} at which the jammer reaches damage $d$.
A jammer is stealthier than another at damage $d$ when its $P^{\star}_\text{det}(d)$ is lower.
The question this work answers is how much lower a learned jammer's $P^{\star}_\text{det}$ is than the lowest one any parametric jammer reaches, for each detector and environment.
Damage is counted in frames because the detectors decide per frame: counted per bit or per symbol, a jammer that is loud on a few frames and silent on the rest reaches any small average error rate while the detectors flag it at their false-alarm rate.
