\section{System Model} \label{sec:sysmodel}

A legitimate transmitter sends \ac{qpsk} frames over a single-carrier link to a receiver that also monitors the link for interference.
A jammer tries to raise the receiver's error rate, and the receiver's detectors try to flag the frames it jams.
The transmitter, the receiver and its detectors stay fixed, and only the jammer adapts.

\subsection{Legitimate Link} \label{sec:sysmodel:link}
Let us define the \textit{frame} as a block of $N$ unit-energy \ac{qpsk} symbols $s[k]\in\{\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\pm1\pm\mathrm{j})\}$, $k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$, drawn independently and uniformly.
The transmitter upsamples each frame by $L$ samples per symbol and shapes it with a unit-energy \ac{rrc} pulse $g$, which gives the transmit waveform
\begin{equation}
x[n]=\sum_{k=1}^{N} s[k]\,g[n-kL].
\label{eq:tx}
\end{equation}
The receiver filters with $g$, samples at the symbol instants to obtain $z_k$, and decides each axis by its sign.
The pulse $g$ is real, even and root-Nyquist, so the matched filter is $g$ itself and the cascade is free of inter-symbol interference up to truncation.
A frame is in error when any of its $2N$ bits is.
The link carries no channel code, preamble or pilot symbols, and the receiver knows its frame timing and carrier phase, so a jammer can corrupt a frame only through its bits, and one wrong symbol suffices.

\subsection{Channel} \label{sec:sysmodel:channel}
The receiver observes
\begin{equation}
r[n]=\rho\,x[n]+c[n]+w[n],\qquad w[n]\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\nu N_0),
\label{eq:rx}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is the jammer's waveform at the receiver and $N_0$ the nominal noise power per sample.
The amplitude $\rho=|h|$, with $h$ Rician with factor $K$ and $\mathbb{E}[\rho^2]=1$, models small-scale fading of the legitimate link.
The factor $\nu$, log-normal with $\mathbb{E}[\nu]=1$ and standard deviation $\sigma_\text{N}$ in decibels, models a noise level that the receiver does not know exactly.
The channel draws $\rho$ and $\nu$ once per frame, and no detector knows them; $K\to\infty$ and $\sigma_\text{N}=0$ give the ideal link.
Every power is a mean per sample at the receiver input.
With $P_\text{rx}$ the mean received signal power, the \ac{snr} is $P_\text{rx}/N_0$ and the symbol energy ratio is $E_\text{s}/N_0=L\,P_\text{rx}/N_0$.

\subsection{Jammer} \label{sec:sysmodel:jammer}
Let us define the \textit{\ac{jsr}} $J$ as the jammer's received power relative to $P_\text{rx}$.
The power budget is a hard constraint on every frame,
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{NL}\sum_{n}|c[n]|^2=J\,P_\text{rx},
\label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
so a jammer can redistribute its power within a frame but cannot move it between frames or trade it against a penalty.

A jammer is asynchronous unless stated otherwise: its timing offset is uniform over one symbol and its carrier phase uniform over $[0,2\pi)$, both drawn per frame.
Four tiers describe what a jammer knows.
A genie (T0) knows the current symbols and the channel gain and arrives synchronously.
A synchronised attacker (T1) knows the receiver's symbol timing and carrier phase but not the symbols.
An informed attacker (T2) knows the modulation, pulse, symbol rate and frame timing, but arrives asynchronously, so it knows the timing only to within one symbol.
A blind attacker (T3) knows none of these.

\subsection{Detectors} \label{sec:sysmodel:detectors}
The receiver tests one frame at a time, without channel state information.
Each detector reduces the frame to a scalar statistic $T$, built so that a larger value is more suspicious, and decides alone.
Let us define the \textit{threshold} $\tau_\alpha$ as the $(1-\alpha)$-quantile of $T$ over clean frames of the environment the detector is deployed in, including the fading and noise draws it cannot know.
The \ac{far} therefore stays at $\alpha$ in every environment, a \ac{cfar} design in which an impairment raises the threshold instead of the false alarms.
Let us define the \textit{detection probability} at \ac{jsr} $J$ as
\begin{equation}
P_\text{det}(J)=\Pr\bigl[T(r)>\tau_\alpha\mid\text{jammer at }J\bigr],
\label{eq:pdet}
\end{equation}
where the probability runs over everything random in the environment: bits, noise, channel, and the jammer's own randomness, delay and phase.
The detection probability is per frame, not the probability that the jammer is ever caught.

The receiver runs three detectors.
The energy detector~\cite{urkowitz1967energy} thresholds the mean symbol energy after the matched filter, $T_\text{E}=\frac1N\sum_k|z_k|^2$, which excludes the noise outside the signal's band and flags excess energy.
Under fading, a gain-aware variant that is handed $\rho$ and removes it from the signal term bounds what a receiver that estimates its gain could achieve.
% energy_csi subtracts (rho^2 - 1) c0^2 (final/detectors.py); a bracket, not a deployable detector.
The kurtosis detector thresholds the deviation of $\kappa(r)=\mathbb{E}|r|^4/(\mathbb{E}|r|^2)^2$ from its clean mean in both directions.
The kurtosis $\kappa$ is scale-free, so it responds to the shape of the amplitude distribution, which energy cannot see.
The learned detector is the spectrogram classifier of Li et al.~\cite{li2022jamming_ofdm}, a \ac{cnn} applied to the two-sided complex \ac{stft} of $r$ on a fixed decibel scale.
Its statistic is the difference of its two output logits.
All three detectors average over the whole frame.

\subsection{Threat Model} \label{sec:sysmodel:threat}
The detectors are calibrated, and the classifier trained, before any attack and then frozen.
A trained jammer is frozen at deployment, so all adaptation is offline.
A white-box jammer differentiates the deployed detector's statistic, including the classifier's weights.
A grey-box jammer knows the classifier's architecture and training recipe but not its weights, and trains against a surrogate of its own~\cite{papernot2017practical}.
For the energy and kurtosis detectors, which have no weights, the two coincide.
A jammer observes the victim's error rate only in simulation, during training.
